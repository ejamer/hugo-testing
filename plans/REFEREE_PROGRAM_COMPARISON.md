# Referee program comparison: FENB and the CFF manual

Checked 2026-11-06. This is an internal working document and is not published with the site.

**Compared:** FENB's process as published at [/programs/referee-development/](https://www.fenb.ca/programs/referee-development/), which is the only FENB definition I could find, against the [CFF Referee Manual, January 2005](https://fencing.ca/wp-content/documents/referee/referee_manual_2005_eng.pdf). Page numbers are PDF pages. If FENB has a fuller internal procedure, some gaps below may already be covered.

## Summary

FENB differs from the manual in one clear way and is silent on several CFF requirements:

1. **Pass mark (conflict).** FENB requires 75% on written exams. The CFF requires 80%.
2. **Practical exam (possible conflict).** The CFF requires 80% at an open domestic ranking competition. FENB states neither a score nor a competition type.
3. **Keeping certification (gap).** The CFF requires a refresher seminar and active refereeing every two years. FENB's page doesn't mention renewal.
4. **Pay levels (internal mismatch).** FENB's pay scale pays a "Regional" level that its program doesn't define, and has no "Club" rate even though its program has that level.

The page now links to the manual, so candidates can see the 75% and 80% figures side by side. The page has no note explaining the difference, so the 75% and 80% figures are unexplained until FENB decides on item 1.

The manual is 21 years old but is still the only one the CFF lists. Before changing FENB's rules to match it, ask the CFF whether its 2005 requirements still apply (see "Is the manual current?").

## Is the manual current?

- **Status:** On 2026-11-06, [fencing.ca/officials](https://fencing.ca/officials/) still listed the 2005 manual in English and French. I found no newer edition, and I didn't contact the CFF.
- **A replacement may be coming:** The [CFF Officials Committee terms of reference](https://fencing.ca/wp-content/uploads/National-Officials-Committee-TOR.pdf), approved 2026-02-08, tell the committee to develop a national training and certification program and to provide guidance to provinces. They also require training documents in both official languages.
- **Dated content:** It mentions "Selection Circuit" events, and its attire rules are gendered. The gesture chart in the English edition is in French (pp. 14–15).
- **US overlap:** The manual's code of ethics is "copied and adapted" from the USFA code (p. 4), so US material is closer than it first appears. Exams and pathways still differ.

## The manual against the current FIE rules

Compared with the [FIE Technical Rules, August 2026](https://fie.org/documents/rules), on the topics the manual discusses. This isn't a full audit of every rule, and the manual doesn't cover newer topics such as video refereeing (t.60–62).

**Where following the manual would lead to a wrong call:**

| Topic | Manual | FIE August 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Corps à corps at foil and sabre | Card the fencer who caused "even the slightest contact" (EN p. 20) | Only causing it intentionally to avoid a hit, or jostling, is penalized (t.25.2). A flèche or vigorous advance that causes it, without brutality or violence, is no fault (t.25.3) |
| Hit scored while covering target | **The English edition** says covering doesn't annul the hit (p. 20). **The French edition** says it does | The hit is annulled (t.29.2, t.79.1). The French edition is right and the English edition is wrong |
| Crossing the side of the piste | Opponent advances one metre when a fencer leaves with both feet (EN p. 22) | One or both feet. A fencer who leaves during an attack first returns to where the attack started, then steps back a metre (t.35.1) |

**Changed wording, same rule:**

| Topic | Manual | FIE August 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Command to start | "Allez" or "Fence" (t.17, t.18) | "Play!" in the English rules (t.22.11, t.23.1) |
| Definition of the attack | t.7 | t.9, nearly the same text |
| Referee's pledge of honour | t.34 | t.46.1, same text |
| Referee's duties | t.35 | t.47 |
| Analysing the phrase | t.42, "reconstructs" | t.59.1, "analyses" |
| Coming on guard | t.17 | t.22 |
| Starting and stopping | t.18 | t.23, same substance |

**Still consistent:** always calling halt on corps à corps (t.25.1), beat on foible or forte (t.85), no coming on guard in line at foil and sabre (t.22.13), a turned-back hit being annulled (t.27.2), and the sabre attack ending when the front foot lands (t.101).

The page now says these calls have changed, names corps à corps, and tells candidates to follow the FIE rules where the two differ. The English manual's covering error may be worth reporting to the CFF.

## Pay scale

The page links to the [FENB Pay Scale](https://docs.google.com/document/d/11ChewHXa5ONLh3k7uNjivKgnuZcby2fVaD4qNUuqHe8/preview) and the HR Policy, and shows no rates, so it can't go out of date when the document changes. The rates below are a snapshot from the date of this report.

**Open questions in FENB's own pay scale:**

- "Regional" exists in the pay scale but not in the program, and "Club" is the reverse.
- "CFF rate" appears for National, International, and workshops, but I couldn't find a published figure.
- "Flat" doesn't say whether it means per day or per tournament.
- The meal allowance guidelines heading has no text under it.

**Comparison with other provinces.** This isn't on the public page. FENB pays referees by the hour, so its daily figures assume an 8-hour day, which is my assumption rather than a stated rate.

| Role | FENB | Manitoba (2023–24) | Ontario (from Jan 2023) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uncertified | $15/h (about $120/day) | $50/day (experienced athlete), $65/day (in training) | $60/day |
| Regional | $20/h (about $160/day) | None | From $80/day |
| Provincial | $25/h (about $200/day) | $110/day | From $110/day |
| National | CFF rate | $150/day | From $150/day |
| FIE | CFF rate | $250/day | From $205/day |
| Secretariat | $150 flat | $120/day, plus $60 pre-competition | $80–$150/day (2019) |
| Armoury | $50 flat | $75–$110/day | $50–$150/day (2019) |
| Mileage | $0.25/km | Not stated | $0.575/km |

The Ontario referee figures are my reading of a table that came through scrambled, with the lowest amount in each row taken as the single-weapon rate. Confirm them with the source before quoting them. The secretariat and armoury figures come from the older 2019 policy. Ontario's rates also vary with the number of weapons held.

**What this suggests:**

- FENB's hourly referee rates look strong, which is useful for recruiting.
- FENB's mileage is under half of Ontario's, which matters given NB travel distances.
- Armoury looks low compared with both provinces.

Sources: [Manitoba pay scale](https://manitobafencing.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/OFficials-Pay-Scale.png), [OFA January 2023 update](https://fencingontario.ca/ofa-officials-committee-updates-january-2023/), [OFA Officials Policy 2019](https://fencingontario.ca/cms/wp-content/uploads/OFA-officials_policy_20190916-1.pdf).

| Idea | Source | Why it might help FENB |

Ontario's example: 10 Regional, 5 Provincial, 5 new recruits |

For reference, other pass marks and fees: Ontario requires 80% and charges $125 (Regional) or $175 (Provincial). Manitoba requires 70% and charges $120. So FENB's 75% isn't unusual, but it is still below the manual's 80%.

## Decisions for FENB

1. Should the written pass mark stay at 75% or move to 80%? Ask the CFF first whether its 80% still applies.
2. What is the score and setting for the provincial practical exam?
3. What are FENB's renewal requirements?
4. Is the out-of-province evaluator still required, and could video evaluation satisfy it?
5. Should the pay scale say "Club" or "Regional"? What are the CFF rates, and what does "flat" cover?
6. Should mileage and armoury rates be reviewed?
